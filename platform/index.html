Platform

Four microkernels. No shared heap.

Software cannot police software. Brad-Core removes the planner from the actuator’s address space. The only thing that crosses is a 64-byte frame. The only thing that can move a pin is Infinium, inside a hard envelope.

Two doctrines. Battleship keeps navigation when the sky is jammed. Emmy Infinity keeps the vehicle when Blade 01 is gone. Neither one lets the planner own the pin.

Battleship Protocol

If primary PNT is jammed, spoofed, or blinded, M-Core leaves it. The handover fits inside one master frame, 625 µs. The shell is annealed OFHC copper outside and mu-metal inside, permeability above 80,000. OptINC carries the data across the hull on light, not copper: more than 10 kV of isolation, 1.20 ns cutoff.

  1. 01 · mesh

    73-node peer mesh

    Every node writes its state into local SHA-256 WORM registers. One compromised node cannot rewrite the fleet.

  2. 02 · sonar

  3. 03 · inertial

    Inertial stellar odometry

    MEMS array, a 3-axis fluxgate, and a starfield. Timing jitter on the loop stays 0.00 ns RMS.

  4. 04 · light

    OptINC across the bulkheads. 1.20 ns propagation. The hull penetration is glass, not wire.

Emmy Infinity · the software twin

The twin is the backup. Its job is to stop a rogue planner from breaching the actuator bus with engineered chaos: a flooded clock, a smeared timestamp, a frame that only looks legal because the loop was shaken. The reverse-chaos clock paralyzes that attack. Sheriff still owns the clamp. The bus stays at 0.00 V. The attempt is written to the WORM ledger. Chaos is not a key.

Blade 02 is the same enclave in hardware, on its own VITA 62 rail, and it is equipped with micro EMP bursts. The planner on Blade 01 does not own that trigger. If the primary chassis loses power, the rail holds 28 V for 120 seconds. Power alone does not continue the mission. Nothing on Blade 02 starts the shadow loop by itself.

Continue mission requires a warrant push packet from a ground operator. A JTAC or a Special Forces operator sends the signed packet, 0xA11F1770_SOF, over OptINC or the mesh. No cloud. No headquarters queue. No packet, no continue.

A valid packet cold-boots a shadow P-Core and a shadow D-Core from WORM ROM in under 53 µs and remaps the actuator pins. The 1,600 Hz loop comes back. The packet does not close the kinetic bus. That still takes the hardware token, the Mode B switch, and the formant. Fail-closed remains 0.00 V on register 0x44.

If the ground link is destroyed, the packet cannot arrive. The mesh and a short-range OptINC path still count, but only if a ground operator is on one of them. When no ground path is left, continue mission is refused. Blade 02 stays up on the rail, the pins stay at 0.00 V, and the refusal is written to the WORM ledger. A lost operator is an alarm. It is not permission.

If the enclave itself is being taken, the keys are zeroized in under 10 µs.
